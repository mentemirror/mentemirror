      <h1>Struggle to flourish</h1>
      <p>Difficulty is not always the opposite of growth. Sometimes it is part of the process through which strength develops.</p>
    </div>
  </header>

  <section class="mm-section">
    <div class="mm-container mm-blog-copy">
      <p>A biology teacher explains to his students that a butterfly will soon struggle to emerge from its cocoon and asks them not to interfere. While the teacher is away, one student sees the butterfly struggling and, out of sympathy, breaks the cocoon open to make the process easier.</p>

      <p>The help has an unintended consequence: the butterfly does not survive. When the teacher returns, he explains that the effort required to come out of the cocoon is itself part of the butterfly's development. The struggle helps its wings become strong enough for what comes next.</p>

      <p>The reflection uses this story to make a broader point about life. Obstacles, disappointments and difficult periods are uncomfortable, but avoiding every struggle does not necessarily make us stronger. People often develop confidence and security by confronting challenges, learning from them and continuing despite discouragement.</p>

      <p>Problems are part of life, and the response proposed by the article is perseverance rather than withdrawal. What initially feels difficult can become manageable with time, effort and repeated attempts.</p>

      <div class="mm-blog-takeaways">
        <article><strong>Struggle can be developmental</strong><p>Some capabilities are built through the very effort required to overcome an obstacle.</p></article>
        <article><strong>Support does not always mean removing difficulty</strong><p>Well-intended help can sometimes take away the opportunity to build confidence and capability.</p></article>
        <article><strong>Perseverance matters</strong><p>Disappointment is common; continuing to engage with the problem is part of moving through it.</p></article>
      </div>
