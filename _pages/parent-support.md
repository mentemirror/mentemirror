        <span class="mm-eyebrow">Support the person, not just the problem</span>
        <h2>Parenting through changing emotional, academic and social needs.</h2>
        <p>Children and adolescents are learning how to manage emotions, relationships, attention, academic pressure, sleep, independence and digital life at the same time. Parent support is designed to help adults respond with greater awareness instead of relying only on correction or crisis response.</p>
        <p>The aim is not to monitor every behaviour. It is to make it easier to recognise patterns, have constructive conversations and create routines that support wellbeing and development.</p>

    <div class="mm-section-heading"><span class="mm-eyebrow">A practical parent approach</span><h2>Notice → Ask → Listen → Support</h2></div>
    <div class="mm-family-process-grid">
      <article><span>01</span><h3>Notice</h3><p>Pay attention to meaningful changes in mood, sleep, routine, concentration, social connection or behaviour.</p></article>
      <article><span>02</span><h3>Ask</h3><p>Start with curiosity and specific observations rather than assumptions.</p></article>
      <article><span>03</span><h3>Listen</h3><p>Give the young person room to describe their experience before moving immediately to solutions.</p></article>
      <article><span>04</span><h3>Support</h3><p>Agree on small practical steps and seek qualified human support when concerns persist or become difficult to manage.</p></article>
    </div>
