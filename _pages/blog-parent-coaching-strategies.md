      <h1>Parent Coaching Strategies</h1>
      <p>Helping children learn from struggle, think ambitiously, build courage and retain kindness.</p>
    </div>
  </header>

  <section class="mm-section">
    <div class="mm-container mm-blog-copy">
      <p>The Mente Mirror article begins from the idea that parents can help children understand that struggles and failures can become important teachers. The goal is not simply to protect a child from difficulty, but to help them become stronger, more confident, courageous and kind while expecting the best from themselves.</p>

      <h2>7 fundamentals of great parenting skills</h2>

      <div class="mm-parenting-principles">
        <article>
          <span>01</span><h2>Teamwork</h2>
          <p>Encourage children to participate in activities and take age-appropriate responsibilities at home. Contributing with others can teach cooperation, reduce self-centred behaviour and show how shared effort strengthens both the individual and the group.</p>
        </article>
        <article>
          <span>02</span><h2>Self-care</h2>
          <p>Help children make room for both responsibilities and recovery. Work time and free time both matter, and learning to balance them helps prevent important areas of life from being neglected.</p>
        </article>
        <article>
          <span>03</span><h2>See possibilities where others see problems</h2>
          <p>Teach children to approach challenges with a belief that solutions can be found. Exploring alternatives, remaining persistent and taking consistent action can turn problems into opportunities for creative thinking.</p>
        </article>
        <article>
          <span>04</span><h2>Motivation</h2>
          <p>Writing goals down can help children define direction. Recognition of genuine effort is also important: encouragement from parents can become a powerful reward and help sustain motivation.</p>
        </article>
        <article>
          <span>05</span><h2>Time management</h2>
          <p>Punctuality and planning are treated as practical life skills. Helping children arrive on time and honour commitments teaches dependability and respect for other people's time.</p>
        </article>
        <article>
          <span>06</span><h2>Accepting responsibility</h2>
          <p>Children benefit from learning to take responsibility for the outcomes of their efforts, whether positive or negative. Reflection helps them recognise what needs to change and adapt their approach rather than repeat the same mistakes.</p>
        </article>
        <article>
          <span>07</span><h2>Kindness</h2>
          <p>The article treats kindness as a strength of character rather than weakness. Parents can create an emotional environment in which consideration for others becomes natural, visible and contagious.</p>
        </article>
      </div>
    </div>
